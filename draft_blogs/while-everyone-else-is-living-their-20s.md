# While Everyone Else Is Living Their 20s

*Vikash V · November 2026*

Sometimes I look at people around my age and wonder what it feels like to just live.

Going to movies without thinking about the work waiting at home. Playing games late into the night because there's nothing particularly urgent the next morning. Meeting friends, falling in love, travelling somewhere, making stupid plans, wasting an entire Sunday.

Just being twenty-something.

And then there's me, sitting in a room within a room, staring at a laptop, trying to figure out what I'm supposed to do next.

I don't even know if I'm doing the right thing half the time.

One day, I'm working on a research problem. Another day, I'm trying to find clients, sending messages to people who probably have better things to do, following up with someone who hasn't replied, or making calls even when I know I'm probably annoying them. Sometimes I can almost hear myself becoming that person who keeps asking, keeps following up, keeps trying to convince someone to give them a chance.

But what else am I supposed to do? Wait for opportunities to magically find me?

There are things to build, people to manage, deadlines to meet, clients to keep happy, college work to finish, and a company that somehow needs to move forward. Everything seems important until everything becomes urgent. And somewhere in between all of that, I'm supposed to make decisions.

The funny part is, I know I'm making mistakes. Not just occasionally. I can see myself making mistakes in almost every direction. Maybe I'm not managing the team properly. Maybe I'm approaching clients the wrong way. Maybe I'm spending too much time on the wrong technical problem. Maybe I should have focused on something else months ago.

I keep switching between responsibilities, knowing damn well that every switch comes with a cost. When I'm working on one thing, I feel guilty about the other four. When I finally finish something, there are already three more things waiting.

And the deadlines don't care.

They don't care that I had a difficult week, that something unexpected happened, or that I spent the previous night trying to fix something that refused to work. They just arrive. One after another.

Sometimes I wonder whether I'm actually building something or just getting very good at staying occupied.

The pressure doesn't come only from work either. It comes from home. From family. From friends who ask why I'm doing so much, why I can't just focus on a stable job, why I'm taking risks when there are easier ways to live. They aren't necessarily wrong. That's what makes it difficult.

Some of them genuinely worry about me. Some don't understand what I'm trying to do. And sometimes, I don't have a convincing explanation myself.

I can't exactly tell them that everything will work out. I don't know that. I can't promise that the company will succeed, that the projects will make money, that all these late nights will eventually mean something. There is no guarantee waiting at the end of this.

I know how it looks from the outside. A young person trying to do too many things at once, constantly talking about projects, startups, research, and opportunities. Maybe it looks exciting. Maybe it even looks like I'm living some extraordinary life.

But there is another side to it.

The side where you sit alone and wonder if you've made a terrible decision. Where you open your laptop because you don't know what else to do. Where you send another client message even though you're embarrassed to send it. Where you have to act confident in front of your team when you're still trying to understand the situation yourself.

And sometimes, I just want to close everything.

I want to go watch a movie without checking my phone every twenty minutes. I want to play a game and actually enjoy it instead of thinking about what I should be doing. I want to meet my friends without mentally calculating how much time I have before I need to get back. I want to have a day that doesn't need to be justified by how productive it was.

I don't think that's laziness. I think I miss being available for my own life.

There's a particular kind of loneliness in choosing a path that the people around you don't fully understand. You can explain what you're building, what you're researching, what you're trying to achieve. But explaining why you're willing to spend so much of your twenties doing it is harder.

And the truth is, I don't always know why either.

Maybe I want to see whether I can actually build something from nothing. Maybe I don't want to look back and wonder what would have happened if I had tried. Maybe I genuinely enjoy solving difficult problems, even when they make me question my entire existence. Maybe I just have too many things I want to do and haven't learned how to let some of them go.

Probably a little of everything.

I don't want to romanticise this. There's nothing particularly beautiful about being constantly worried, feeling behind, or knowing that people are depending on decisions you're not entirely confident about. Hard work doesn't automatically make someone successful. Sacrifice doesn't guarantee a meaningful outcome. And being busy is not the same as moving forward.

I know all of that.

But I'm here anyway.

I see people enjoying their twenties, and honestly, I'm happy they get to. I don't want them to stop. I don't think there's anything wrong with going out, playing games, watching movies, falling in love, or doing absolutely nothing for a weekend. If anything, I wish I did more of those things.

I just sometimes wonder whether I'll get to experience that version of life without feeling like I'm falling behind.

Maybe one day I'll have a little more stability. Maybe the things I'm building will find their place. Maybe I'll learn to manage my time, my responsibilities, and my expectations better. Or maybe I'll realise that I was trying to carry too much and that some things could have waited.

I don't know.

For now, there are still messages to send, people to call, work to finish, decisions to make, and deadlines that won't move just because I'm tired.

So I'll probably go back to that room within a room.

Not because I have everything figured out. Not because I'm certain this is the right path. And definitely not because I don't want to be out there enjoying life.

I just hope that somewhere along the way, while trying so hard to build a future, I don't completely miss the years I'm living right now.

Because these are my twenties too.

And I want to be able to say that I lived them, not just worked through them.
