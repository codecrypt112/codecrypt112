# Having No Choice But to Do It

*by Vikash V · November 2026*

---

Sometimes I wonder what would happen if I just stopped.

Not quit everything. Not disappear. Just stopped for a while.

No deadlines. No research papers waiting to be read. No production issues. No startup ideas. No half-finished experiments sitting somewhere, waiting for me to return.

Just a few days of doing absolutely nothing.

I wonder how that would feel.

And then reality kicks in.

There are things waiting. People depending on me. Things I've started that I can't simply walk away from. Responsibilities that don't disappear just because I'm tired.

So I get back to work.

Not because I'm always motivated. Not because I wake up every morning excited to do all of this.

Sometimes, I simply don't feel like I have another option.

---

I don't think people understand the difference between *wanting to do something* and *feeling like you have to do it.*

They look similar from the outside.

You see someone working late, building products, studying, doing research, taking up new challenges. It looks like ambition. Maybe it is.

But there's another side to it.

Sometimes you're working because you know what happens if you don't.

Sometimes you're learning something new because you can't afford to remain where you are.

Sometimes you're taking up another responsibility because the opportunity might not come again.

And sometimes you keep going because you've already come too far to imagine what stopping would look like.

It's a strange place to be.

You aren't exactly unhappy with what you're doing.

But you aren't entirely free from it either.

---

There have been days when I wanted to close my laptop and walk away.

Days when a research problem refused to make sense, a project wasn't moving, and everything I had planned for the day remained unfinished.

You look at the clock.

Another day gone.

And somehow, you feel guilty for being tired.

That's probably the part I struggle with the most.

Rest feels like something I need to earn.

I finish one task, and another appears. I solve one problem, and three more show up. Even when nothing is urgent, there's always something I could be doing.

There's always another paper.

Another concept.

Another feature.

Another opportunity I might be missing.

I don't know when I started treating every quiet moment as something that needed to be filled.

---

Maybe some of it comes from where I started.

When you don't have everything figured out, opportunities feel different.

You don't casually turn them down.

You don't always get to choose the perfect project, the perfect timing, or the perfect circumstances.

You learn what you can.

You take what comes your way.

You figure things out as you go.

And somewhere in that process, you become the person people expect to figure things out.

The person who can take a difficult problem and somehow make progress.

The person who says, "I'll try."

I like being that person.

But sometimes I wish people understood that figuring things out doesn't mean I always know what I'm doing.

Sometimes I'm just as lost as everyone else.

I just don't have the luxury of staying lost for too long.

---

There's a particular kind of loneliness in doing things nobody around you fully understands.

You can explain your job.

You can explain your startup.

You can tell someone you're working on a research problem.

But explaining why you're doing all of them at once is harder.

People ask why you don't slow down.

Some ask why you take on so much.

Others tell you that you're young and should enjoy life.

They're probably right.

I know there are things I'm missing.

Conversations I should have stayed longer for. Friends I should have called back. Evenings that could have been spent doing absolutely nothing.

I notice these things.

It's not that I don't care.

Sometimes I care so much that I don't know what to put down first.

---

I wish I could say there's some beautiful philosophy behind all of this.

That I have a clear vision, a perfect plan, and an unshakable belief in where I'm going.

I don't.

There are days when I question almost everything.

Whether I'm moving fast enough.

Whether I'm learning the right things.

Whether all these efforts will eventually lead somewhere.

Whether I'm building something meaningful or just keeping myself occupied.

I don't always have answers.

But the next morning comes anyway.

There's work to do.

And I get up.

---

Maybe that's what this is really about.

Not being endlessly motivated.

Not being exceptionally disciplined.

Not even being ambitious all the time.

It's about having something inside you that refuses to let you walk away.

Maybe it's curiosity.

Maybe it's responsibility.

Maybe it's the fear of going back to a place you've spent years trying to move beyond.

Maybe it's the hope that all these seemingly disconnected efforts will eventually make sense.

I honestly don't know.

Perhaps it's a little bit of everything.

---

I still want to build things.

I still want to understand difficult problems, work on ideas that seem a little too ambitious, and see what I'm capable of.

I don't want to lose that part of myself.

But I'm beginning to understand that wanting a life and being consumed by the pursuit of it are two different things.

I haven't figured out where that line is.

I'm still trying.

And tomorrow, I'll probably open my laptop again.

Not because I have everything figured out.

Not because I'm ready.

But because there are things I still want to do, things I need to do, and a future I'm not quite ready to give up on.

Sometimes, that's all the reason you have.

And sometimes, that's enough.

---

*Some things we do because we love them. Some because we have to. And sometimes, after doing them long enough, we can no longer tell the difference.*
