# The Fear of Being Responsible
*Vikash V · November 2026*

There is a strange kind of fear that comes with having a team.

It's not the fear of failing at something yourself. I've experienced that plenty of times. Projects fail, experiments don't work, ideas turn out to be bad. You take a hit, learn something, and try again.

But when you have people working with you, failure starts to feel different.

Because now, it isn't just your time you're spending. It's theirs too.

Their effort. Their expectations. Sometimes, even their trust in you.

And that's a responsibility I don't think I fully understood until I had to carry it.

I still have moments where I look at everything and wonder whether I'm actually ready for this.

Am I making the right decisions? Am I giving the team enough direction? Do I know how to handle disagreements? What happens when something goes wrong and everyone looks at me for an answer?

There are days when I don't even have an answer for myself.

Yet, somehow, I'm expected to find one for everyone else.

That's probably one of the strangest parts of building something with other people. You don't get to wait until you feel completely confident. There are conversations to have, decisions to make, things to resolve. Even when you're uncertain, the work keeps moving.

And you have to move with it.

I sometimes feel like I'm learning two things at the same time: how to build the thing we're working on, and how to become someone people can depend on while building it.

The second one is much harder.

Nobody really prepares you for the small things that come with managing people. Following up without sounding like you're constantly checking on them. Knowing when to give someone space and when to step in. Making decisions that won't make everyone happy. Admitting that you don't know something without making the entire team feel lost.

And then there is the question that stays in the back of my mind:

What if I let them down?

It's easy to talk about leadership when everything is going well. When deadlines are met, ideas work, and everyone is moving in the same direction.

But what about when there isn't enough progress? When plans change? When you cannot provide the opportunities or resources you hoped to provide? When someone has put their time into something and you are still trying to figure out whether it will work?

I think about those things more than I probably show.

I don't want to become the kind of person who gives people responsibilities without understanding the weight of them. I don't want to make promises just because they sound good. And I definitely don't want someone else's effort to become something I take for granted.

At the same time, I know I will make mistakes.

I'm still learning. I'm still figuring out how to communicate better, how to plan properly, how to make decisions, and how to handle situations I've never encountered before.

Sometimes I feel too young for the responsibilities I've taken on. Other times, I wonder whether age has anything to do with it. Maybe everyone who starts something new feels a little unqualified at first.

But the fear doesn't go away just because I acknowledge it.

I still feel it before certain conversations. Before making decisions. Before telling someone that we're changing direction. Before taking responsibility for something that might not work.

And yet, I show up.

Not because I'm fearless. Not because I have some exceptional ability to lead people. I show up because I know there are people who have chosen to spend their time building something alongside me. I owe them honesty, effort, and the willingness to take responsibility when things get difficult.

I don't think bravery in this situation means acting like you know everything.

Maybe it means being honest when you don't. Listening when someone has a better idea. Taking responsibility when a decision goes wrong instead of looking for someone to blame. And making sure that the people working with you don't have to carry your mistakes alone.

I'm beginning to understand that managing a team isn't just about dividing work and tracking progress.

It's about trust.

And trust is something you have to earn repeatedly. Not through big speeches or promises, but through the ordinary things you do every day.

I still get scared about what I'm responsible for. I still question whether I'm doing enough. I still have a lot to learn.

But I don't want that fear to become a reason to avoid responsibility.

Because these aren't just people helping me build something. They're people choosing to be part of it. And I want to become someone worthy of that choice.

Maybe I'll become a better leader with time. Maybe I'll look back at the way I manage things today and realise how much I got wrong.

For now, I'm learning.

And perhaps that's what bravery looks like at this stage of my life.

Not having everything under control.

Just being afraid, understanding what's at stake, and choosing to show up anyway.
