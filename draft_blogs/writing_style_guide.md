# Vikash's Writing & Blogging Style Guide

This guide codifies the writing style, structure, and tone used in my personal blog drafts. Keep this reference in mind for future posts or revisions to match the voice exactly.

---

## 1. Pacing and Structure

- **Breathable Layout:** Use frequent paragraph breaks. Almost every sentence or thought gets its own line. Avoid dense blocks of text.
- **Rhythmic Beats:** Alternate between ultra-short fragments (1-3 words) and moderate sentences to create a natural, spoken flow.
  
  *Example:*
  > It's **1:47 AM**.
  > 
  > The world is quiet.
  > 
  > My screen isn't.

- **Whitespace as Punctuation:** Empty lines are used strategically to let thoughts settle, build tension, or mark a transition in mindset.

---

## 2. Tone and Voice

- **Unfiltered & Conversational:** Speak directly, like writing in a private journal or explaining something to a friend late at night. No academic or overly polished phrasing.
- **Quiet Vulnerability:** Acknowledge the weight of the routine, the mistakes, the guilt, and the exhaustion without self-pity or melodrama.
- **Reluctant Drive:** The focus isn't on bragging about the hustle. It’s about an unavoidable itch to build:
  
  *Example:*
  > Curiosity has always been louder than comfort.
  > 
  > So I pick it up.

- **Realistic Imagery:** Use tangible metaphors that represent the developer/creator experience ("screws slowly coming loose," "fresh terminal window," "cold coffee," "green squares").

---

## 3. Formatting Rules

- **Sparse Bold Text:** Use bold primarily for timestamps (`**1:47 AM**`), key realizations, or framing specific numbers.
- **Short Fragment Lists:** When listing occurrences, actions, or consequences, lay them out line-by-line rather than separating them with commas.
  
  *Example:*
  > Some mistakes cost sleep.
  > 
  > Some cost money.
  > 
  > Some cost confidence.

- **Natural Code & App Integration:** Reference tools, folder names, or apps exactly as they are seen (`Proj`, `Cliq`, `GitHub`) without explaining what they are to the reader.
- **Ellipses and Pauses:** Use `...` at the end of transition lines to mimic the natural pauses of deep thought or hesitation.
